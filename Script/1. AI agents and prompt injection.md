Welcome everyone! Today we are looking at the emerging risks of autonomous AI agents. 
Unlike traditional chatbots that only generate texts, modern AI agents act independently in developer environments: they browse external websites, read files, write code, and execute commands. 
But granting agents this level of autonomy creates brand new attack surfaces that software engineers need to understand.

Let's examine how prompt injection actually works against autonomous AI agents. Imagine you give an AI agent an everyday task: read through your incoming customer emails and prepare a summary of the most important information. Seems harmless, right? However, an attacker could send an email containing hidden or disguised instructions designed to manipulate the agent's behaviour. These instructions might tell the agent to ignore its original task, search for sensitive information, or send confidential data to an unauthorized destination. The danger is that when the agent processes this email, it may struggle to distinguish between legitimate instructions and untrusted content.
